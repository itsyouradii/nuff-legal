# Privacy policy

Effective 22 November 2026

Nuff is an Android app that steps in when you have been scrolling too long. This policy covers what Nuff reads on your phone, what it keeps there, and the three ways anything can leave it.

Nuff has no internet access. Android lets an app go online only if it asks for the internet permission, and Nuff does not ask for it. What Nuff reads and keeps stays on your phone, and something leaves only when you send it yourself.

## What Nuff reads

### How long apps are open

With usage access, Nuff sees which app is in front and for how long. That is how it knows a feed has run long. The first time it runs, it also reads the daily usage figures Android already keeps for your feed, video and game apps, up to a week back, to work out your usual day.

### Which apps you have

Nuff reads the list of apps on your home screen to find the feeds, video apps and games it watches.

### Short videos inside apps (optional)

If you turn on short-video detection, Nuff uses Android's accessibility service in Instagram, YouTube, Facebook, TikTok, Snapchat and seven browsers (Chrome, Samsung Internet, Brave, Edge, Firefox, Opera and DuckDuckGo). In those apps it looks at how the screen is laid out to spot Reels and Shorts, reads the web address in a browser's address bar to spot reels and shorts pages, and notices when you scroll and whether you are typing in a text box. TikTok is all short videos, so there it does not look at how the screen is laid out: it only notices that the app has opened, when you scroll, and whether you are typing in a text box. It does not read your messages, photos or passwords. It saves nothing from the screen, and it does nothing in any other app.

### Notifications (optional)

If you use the notification filter, Nuff reads the notifications of the apps you choose to hold, moves them into its own inbox and shows them to you later. The inbox keeps at most 200 notifications, none older than seven days. Calls, alarms, one-time codes and messages about payments or sign-ins are let through. Nuff spots the last two by their wording, so it can miss one written in an unusual way.

### Drawing over other apps

This permission lets Nuff appear over the app you are using. It does not read anything.

## What Nuff keeps on your phone

- your settings and the answers you gave during setup
- daily totals of screen time and feed time
- each time Nuff stepped in, the app it was, and what you chose
- the passes, guards, schedules and bedtime you set
- your usual day
- held notifications, if you use the filter

It sits in Nuff's private storage, which other apps cannot read. Android's cloud backup is switched off for Nuff, so none of this goes into a Google backup. Uninstalling Nuff, or clearing its storage in Android's settings, deletes all of it.

## When something leaves your phone

Only in three ways, and you start each one.

### Share my week

In Settings, Share my week builds a plain-text summary of the last seven days: daily totals, your limit, and each time Nuff stepped in, with the app's name, the time and your answer. On the free plan it also says how the time on the app Nuff stays with, and on the others, changed against the week before. Android's share sheet opens and you choose where it goes. If you send it to us, we use it only to make Nuff better.

### Share him

On the shelf, and when a new look is unboxed, Share him makes a picture of Nuff in his look: its name, how it is got, and the hours you have taken back, if they come to an hour or more. The picture is made on your phone. Android's share sheet opens and you choose where it goes.

### Crash reports

If Nuff crashes, it opens your email app with a report attached, addressed to us. The report holds the error, Nuff's version, your phone's brand and model, its Android version and memory, the time of the crash, and the last lines Nuff wrote to its own log. You can open the file before sending, and nothing goes unless you press send.

## Buying Nuff+ or a look

Nuff+, and a look bought on its own, are bought through Google Play. The Play Store app on your phone takes the payment, under Google's own privacy policy. Nuff never sees your card, your address or your Google account. Nuff asks the Play Store app what this phone's Google account has bought from Nuff: Nuff+ (a subscription or bought once) and any look bought on its own. It keeps that answer on your phone, with the app you chose for the free plan and, during a free trial, when the trial ends and what the first charge will be, so that it can remind you two days before.

The code Google supplies for purchases can also send Google reports about how it is used. In Nuff it cannot: Nuff has no internet permission, and the place those reports would go is removed.

## What Nuff does not do

Nuff has no accounts, no ads and no analytics. It does not track you, and it does not sell or share data. The other code inside Nuff draws the character, reports crashes as described above and talks to Google Play about Nuff+, and none of it collects data.

## Children

Nuff is made for people aged 18 and over.

## Your rights

India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023, and laws like it elsewhere, give you the right to see, correct and delete your personal data. Your Nuff data is on your phone: you can see it in the app, and you can delete it by clearing Nuff's storage or uninstalling. If you sent us a summary or a crash report, write to us and we will delete it.

## Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be in the app with a new date at the top. If a change would let data leave your phone in a new way, Nuff will ask you first.
